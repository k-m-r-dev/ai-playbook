The hours do not rise. They rest side by side.

A week earlier, on the bus out of Daulatpur, Nabila had kept a torn copy of Tagore's The Postmaster under her arm and had watched her cousin short a fistful of weight at the bazaar stall. She was afraid, and she used that word. She took the spare key and did not settle whether it was for the customers or for herself. The post-office locker holds a second ledger, a debt to a printer in Kushtia, and that debt is the other track. She does not return the key and she does not forgive the taking.

And in the end the post office held, as the stories before it had held, a small quiet inside a long tradition, and the rain was only rain, and Nabila was, simply, still there.
