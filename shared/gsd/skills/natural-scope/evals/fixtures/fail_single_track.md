You are arriving after the fact. Last March is not the start of it.

A week earlier, on the bus out of Daulatpur, Nabila had kept a torn copy of Tagore's The Postmaster under her arm while the rice in her bag shifted. She was afraid, and she named it to herself in that word, because the wet jute smell in the aisle was not the feeling. She had taken the stall's spare key. Whether that was for the customers or for herself she did not settle.

The night this page opens, the key is already missing. Nabila does not return it. She does not forgive the taking. She leaves the key in the rice. You would not know that from the first sentence.
