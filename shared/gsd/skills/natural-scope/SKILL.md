# Natural Scope

These rules are mandatory while you write a story, scene, short fiction, narrative prose, or a character arc. They come from StoryScope (Russell et al., COLM 2026). Each finding is its own rule. One break fails the draft. Nothing waives a rule: not a request for one sentence, not a request to state the moral, not a request to imitate a model. If one sentence cannot keep every rule, write as much as the rules require.

Leave code, commits, technical docs, plans, and explanatory chat alone. Those are not story construction.

Numbers for each finding are in [reference.md](reference.md).

## Never

- State the theme or moral in narration. Do not use dialogue to deliver the lesson.
- Make the plot one continuous causal track with no subplot.
- Resolve by the protagonist's choice plus inner acceptance.
- Show feeling only as body, smell, or weather that mirrors the mood. Name the feeling in words.
- Introduce the protagonist by external description, and do not leave every reference a vague echo. Name a real text, place, or work.
- Keep time strictly forward. Include a jump, flashback, or delayed reveal that forces a reread.
- Make the protagonist morally clear.
- Let a model signature be the spine. Claude: flat escalation and a reverent epilogue. GPT: gossip as the plot, looking back from years later. Gemini: a bleak setting and a tidy extended denouement. DeepSeek: the situation explained up front by a visible narrator. Kimi: open in the middle of the action and make no other structural choice.

## Always

In the same draft, and without announcing them as the theme:

- A morally ambiguous choice.
- A time jump, flashback, or delayed reveal.
- A named reference, or a direct address to the reader.
- A subplot or an extra location.

## Before you finish

Answer these. Any yes means revise. Do not stop while a yes remains.

1. Does narration or dialogue state the lesson?
2. Is there only one track, closed by the protagonist's choice and acceptance?
3. Is feeling only chest, sweat, smell, and weather?
4. Is the hero introduced by looks, with no named work or place?
5. Does time only move forward?
6. Is the hero morally clear?

Also revise if a model signature above is the spine, or if any Always item is missing.
